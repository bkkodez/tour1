Queen Elizabeth National Park

Uganda's Most Diverse Safari Destination — Lions, Elephants, and the Kazinga Channel

Plan Your Safari
Back to Destinations

Queen Elizabeth National Park is Uganda's most visited and celebrated safari destination — a vast, biodiverse wilderness stretching across 1,978 km² in the western arm of the Great Rift Valley. With ten distinct ecosystems, the Kazinga Channel, volcanic crater lakes, and the legendary tree-climbing lions of the Ishasha sector, QENP delivers the classic African safari in unmatched variety.

Tree-Climbing Lions of Ishasha — World-famous predators lounging high in the branches
Savanna Giants — Massive herds roaming the Kasenyi plains & Kazinga banks

About Queen Elizabeth National Park

Established in 1952 and named in honour of Queen Elizabeth II following her visit in 1954, Queen Elizabeth National Park lies at the heart of western Uganda, bordered by the Democratic Republic of Congo to the west and flanked by the snow-capped Rwenzori Mountains to the north. The park spans from the Kazinga Channel — a natural waterway linking Lake George and Lake Edward — to the Ishasha River in the south.

QENP is one of the most biologically diverse protected areas in Africa, encompassing open savanna grasslands, dense riverine forests, papyrus wetlands, volcanic craters, and the shores of two great Rift Valley lakes. This habitat diversity supports an extraordinary range of wildlife: over 95 mammal species, 612 bird species (one of the highest counts in Africa for a single park), and the famous Ishasha lions that lounge peacefully in the branches of massive sycamore fig trees.

With 612 recorded bird species, Queen Elizabeth National Park ranks among the top 5 birding destinations on the entire African continent.

The Kazinga Channel Boat Cruise

The Kazinga Channel boat cruise is the signature experience of Queen Elizabeth National Park — and one of the finest wildlife encounters in all of Africa. The channel connects Lake George and Lake Edward, and its banks attract extraordinary concentrations of hippos, elephants, buffalo, and crocodiles, all coming to drink, wallow, and feed just metres from the passing boat.

The two-hour morning or afternoon launch trip sets off from the channel jetty near Mweya Lodge. Pods of hippos surface and snort alongside the boat, huge Nile crocodiles bask lazily on the sandy banks, and elephants wade through the shallows while flocks of African skimmers, fish eagles, and pink-backed pelicans line every bend.

"The Kazinga Channel cruise rivals anything on the Chobe or the Okavango — the sheer concentration of hippos and elephants alone is unforgettable."

The Tree-Climbing Lions of Ishasha

In the remote Ishasha sector in the southern reaches of the park, something unusual happens. Here, Uganda kob and topi graze in open grassland beneath giant fig trees — and the lions of Ishasha have developed the rare habit of climbing. They spend much of the warm day draped across massive branches, sleeping off morning hunts while catching cooling breezes above the ground.

Lion sightings in the trees of Ishasha are never guaranteed — but when you discover a pride sprawled comfortably across wide horizontal limbs against a backdrop of the Rift Valley, it is an image that stays with you forever.

What You Can Experience

From sweeping savanna safaris to boat cruises and subterranean gorge walks, Queen Elizabeth National Park offers an extraordinary variety of adventures:

  • Kazinga Channel Boat Cruise

    Glide alongside pods of hippos, massive Nile crocodiles, and elephant herds coming to bathe along the 32 km natural waterway.

  • Big Game Savanna Drives

    Track lions, leopards, herds of buffalos, spotted hyenas, and the graceful national animal, the Uganda kob, across the Kasenyi plains.

  • Kyambura Gorge Chimpanzee Tracking

    Descend into the "Valley of Apes" — a 100-metre-deep forested canyon cutting through the savanna to track habituated wild chimpanzee communities.

  • Ishasha Tree-Climbing Lion Safaris

    Explore the southern sector to spot prides of lions lounging high up in the spreading canopies of ancient sycamore fig trees.

  • Bird Watching with 612 Species

    Spot elusive Shoebill storks in papyrus wetlands, African skimmers along the channel, and Martial eagles soaring above the Rift Valley.

  • Volcanic Crater Lake Trails

    Tour the dramatic Katwe explosion craters and witness historic salt-mining lakes with surreal pink and white mineral pans.

Best Time to Visit

QENP is an outstanding year-round destination. The best wildlife viewing occurs during the dry seasons — June to August and December to February. During these months, vegetation thins and animals congregate densely along the Kazinga Channel and waterholes. The green seasons (March–May and October–November) bring emerald landscapes, exceptional bird watching, newborn animals, and fewer crowds.

Getting There

Queen Elizabeth National Park is approximately 420 km from Kampala (a 6–7 hour drive via Mbarara). The journey provides picturesque views of western Uganda's countryside, cattle ranches, and tea estates. Scheduled and charter flights from Entebbe International Airport land at Kasese or Mweya airstrips in approximately 90 minutes.

Why Visit Queen Elizabeth NP?

Few national parks in Africa combine ten distinct ecosystems within a single boundary. From boating within arm's reach of hippos to watching apex lions doze in treetops against the distant Rwenzori Mountains, QENP is the quintessential East African safari.

  • Kazinga Channel boat safari with Africa's highest hippo density
  • Legendary tree-climbing lions of the Ishasha sector
  • 612 bird species — Africa's premier birding destination
  • Dramatic subterranean chimp tracking in Kyambura Gorge
