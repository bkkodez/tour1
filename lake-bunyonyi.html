Lake Bunyonyi

The Place of Little Birds — 29 Islands of Pure Tranquility in Southwestern Uganda

Plan Your Safari
Back to Destinations

Tucked away in the terraced green amphitheatre of the Kigezi highlands near the Rwanda border, Lake Bunyonyi is Africa's second deepest lake — a serene, mirror-like expanse dotted with 29 lush islands. Wholly free of bilharzia, hippos, and crocodiles, it is Uganda's premier lakeside haven for tranquil dugout canoeing, scenic swimming, and post-gorilla trek rejuvenation.

The Place of Little Birds — 29 lush islands resting in Africa's second deepest lake
Emerald Terraces — Centuries of Bakiga hillside cultivation surrounding the lake

About Lake Bunyonyi

Lake Bunyonyi ("Place of Little Birds" in the native Rukiga language) is an ancient flooded river valley formed thousands of years ago when volcanic lava flows from the nearby Virunga mountains blocked the regional river system. Nestled at an elevation of 1,962 metres above sea level between Kabale and Kisoro, the lake stretches 25 km in length with estimated depths of up to 900 metres.

Crucially for travellers, Lake Bunyonyi is entirely devoid of bilharzia parasites, Nile crocodiles, and hippos, making its crisp, mountain-fresh waters perfectly safe for open-water swimming. Surrounded by intensive terrace farming practiced for generations by the Bakiga people, it offers a peaceful, temperate oasis unlike anywhere else in East Africa.

Lake Bunyonyi is 100% free of bilharzia, hippos, and crocodiles — making it one of the rare freshwater lakes in sub-Saharan Africa safe for swimming and water sports.

The 29 Islands & Historic Lore

Twenty-nine distinct emerald islands dot the surface of Lake Bunyonyi, each enveloped in rich regional folklore. Exploring these islands in a handcrafted wooden dugout canoe (bwato) or on a leisurely motorboat cruise is the defining adventure of the lake.

Among the most famous is Akampene (Punishment Island) — a tiny islet with a solitary tree where, in centuries past, unmarried pregnant Bakiga women were tragically abandoned according to strict tribal taboos, often saved by men who lacked the wealth to pay bride prices. Nearby, Bushara Island offers tranquil forest walking trails, while Njuyeera (Sharp's Island) retains the historic stone cottage and botanical gardens built in 1931 by missionary Dr. Leonard Sharp.

"Gliding silently across Lake Bunyonyi at dawn in a carved wooden canoe as morning mist rolls off the terraced hills is pure, unfiltered peace."

What You Can Experience

Whether seeking pure relaxation after a demanding gorilla trek or engaging cultural explorations, Lake Bunyonyi provides a refreshing escape:

  • Traditional Dugout Canoeing (Bwato)

    Learn to paddle handcrafted wooden dugout canoes across glassy waters guided by friendly local islanders.

  • 29-Island Hopping Cruises

    Visit historic Punishment Island (Akampene), Bushara bird sanctuary, and the leprosy colony history of Bwama Island.

  • Bilharzia-Free Freshwater Swimming

    Swim safely off private resort wooden docks and sun terraces into fresh, cool mountain water without health hazards.

  • Over 200 Bird Species (Place of Little Birds)

    Spot grey crowned cranes, pied kingfishers, African fish eagles, and bright weaver bird colonies along the papyrus fringe.

  • Terraced Hilltop Hiking & Viewpoints

    Trek the steep Kigezi ridges for bird's-eye panoramic vistas across the maze of islands and distant Virunga volcanoes.

  • Bakiga & Batwa Community Tours

    Visit traditional local homesteads, school programmes, and Batwa community cultural performances supporting local education.

Best Time to Visit

Thanks to its high altitude of nearly 2,000 metres, Lake Bunyonyi enjoys mild, spring-like weather (15–25°C) all year round without oppressive heat. The driest months — June to August and December to February — offer sparkling clear reflections, crisp sunny mornings, and ideal conditions for canoeing and swimming.

Getting There

Lake Bunyonyi is located just 8 km (a 20-minute drive) from Kabale town in southwestern Uganda, roughly 410 km from Kampala (6–7 hours drive). It serves as the ultimate post-trek relaxation base, situated just 1.5 to 2 hours drive south of Bwindi Impenetrable Forest and 1.5 hours from Mgahinga Gorilla National Park.

Why Visit Lake Bunyonyi?

Often called the "Switzerland of Africa," Lake Bunyonyi is the quintessential place to unwind, soak in breathtaking island scenery, and reflect on an unforgettable mountain gorilla encounter.
