Murchison Falls National Park

Where the Nile Plunges Through a 7-Metre Gap — Africa's Mightiest Waterfall

Plan Your Safari
Back to Destinations

Murchison Falls National Park is Uganda's largest and oldest protected area — an immense 3,893 km² wilderness in the northwest where the entire Victoria Nile forces itself through a dramatic 7-metre rock crevice before exploding 43 metres into the Albertine Rift below. Beyond the roar of the falls, the park presents East Africa's finest classic big game safaris across golden savanna plains.

The World's Most Powerful Waterfall — The entire Nile squeezed through a 7-metre gorge
Endangered Nubian Giraffes — Uganda's only wild population roaming the North Bank

About Murchison Falls National Park

Established in 1952, Murchison Falls National Park (historically known as Kabalega National Park) sprawls across 3,893 km² in northwestern Uganda, straddling the Victoria Nile as it carves its way from Lake Victoria northward to Lake Albert. Together with Budongo Forest Reserve to the south and Karuma Wildlife Reserve to the east, it forms one of East Africa's most significant and diverse conservation landscapes.

The park takes its name from the legendary waterfall at its heart — where the Nile is violently compressed through a cleft just 7 metres wide before plunging 43 metres into the churning cauldron below in a deafening torrent of white water, rainbow spray, and ground-trembling thunder.

Murchison Falls is the most powerful waterfall in the world: the entire discharge of Africa's longest river compressed into a narrow 7-metre chasm before dropping into the Devil's Cauldron.

The Falls & The Nile Boat Cruise

The launch trip from Paraa jetty to the base of the falls is Murchison's quintessential safari experience — a three-hour boat voyage upstream along the Victoria Nile past dense pods of snorting hippos, basking Nile crocodiles up to 5 metres long, and family herds of elephants bathing in the shallows.

Rounding the final bend on the river, the full drama of the falls comes into view. For adventurous travellers, stepping ashore at the base to embark on the 45-minute guided hike to the top of the falls provides breathtaking panoramic views down into the churning froth of the Devil's Cauldron.

"Standing at the crest of Murchison Falls and feeling the mist on your face while the entire River Nile crashes beneath your boots is one of the most awe-inspiring experiences in nature."

North Bank Big Game Drives

The rolling grasslands of the North Bank deliver Uganda's premier open-savanna game viewing. Morning and sunset drives across the delta tracks reveal healthy prides of lions, leopards resting in acacia trees, massive herds of Cape buffalo, African elephants, Jackson's hartebeests, oribis, and spotted hyenas.

Crucially, Murchison Falls protects Uganda's only surviving population of the endangered Nubian (Rothschild's) giraffe — the tallest subspecies on Earth — with herds gracefully moving against the acacia-studded horizon.

What You Can Experience

From boat cruises to big game tracking and forest chimp encounters, Murchison offers an unforgettable safari portfolio:

  • Victoria Nile Boat Cruise

    Cruise from Paraa to the roaring base of the falls past giant Nile crocodiles, pods of hippos, and bathing elephant herds.

  • Top of the Falls Guided Hike

    Hike from the river's edge up the canyon to witness the river compressing through the narrow cleft with thunderous roar and spray.

  • North Bank Big Game Drives

    Search for lions, leopards, Nubian giraffes, elephants, and Jackson's hartebeest across the vast savanna plains.

  • Budongo Forest Chimpanzee Tracking

    Venture into the ancient mahogany canopy of Kaniyo Pabidi to track wild habituated chimpanzees and rare forest primates.

  • Albert Delta Shoebill Birding

    Take a dedicated morning boat cruise into the papyrus swamps where the Victoria Nile meets Lake Albert to spot the rare prehistoric Shoebill.

  • World-Class Nile Perch Sport Fishing

    Battle massive Nile perch weighing up to 100 kg in the turbulent waters below the falls on catch-and-release sport fishing safaris.

Best Time to Visit

Murchison Falls National Park is a premier year-round destination. The primary dry seasons — December to February and June to August — offer optimal wildlife viewing as grasses are shorter and animals gather along the Nile banks. The green seasons (March–May and October–November) bring emerald landscapes, vibrant bird migrations, and quieter trails.

Getting There

Murchison Falls is situated approximately 305 km northwest of Kampala (a 4.5 to 6 hour drive along the Gulu highway). Most itineraries include a stop at the Ziwa Rhino Sanctuary en route for on-foot white rhino tracking. Daily domestic charter flights operate from Entebbe International Airport directly to Pakuba, Bugungu, or Chobe airstrips in 60 minutes.

Why Visit Murchison Falls?

From the raw geological force of the Nile rushing through a 7-metre gap to sweeping open savannas populated by giraffes and lions, Murchison Falls is the grandest wildlife spectacle in Uganda.
