Bwindi Impenetrable Forest

Uganda's Ancient Gorilla Kingdom — Where Man Meets Mountain Gorilla

Plan Your Safari
Back to Destinations

Bwindi Impenetrable National Park is one of Africa's most extraordinary wilderness areas — a UNESCO World Heritage Site blanketing 331 km² of ancient rainforest in southwestern Uganda. It is home to roughly half of the world's remaining mountain gorillas, making it the premier destination for gorilla trekking on the planet.

The Ancient Canopy — 25,000 years of unbroken biodiversity

About Bwindi Impenetrable Forest

Bwindi Impenetrable Forest sits on the edge of the Albertine Rift in the Kigezi highlands of southwestern Uganda, at elevations ranging from 1,160 to 2,607 metres above sea level. The park's name — "impenetrable" — tells its own story: a dense, primordial tangle of ancient trees, hanging vines, rushing streams, and mist-draped ridges that has remained virtually undisturbed for over 25,000 years.

Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994, Bwindi protects one of the richest ecosystems in Africa. The forest holds over 120 mammal species, more than 350 bird species (including 23 Albertine Rift endemics), 220 butterfly species, and hundreds of plant species found nowhere else on Earth.

Bwindi is home to approximately 459 mountain gorillas — roughly half of the entire world population — across 19 habituated gorilla families available for trekking.

Gorilla Trekking — The Experience of a Lifetime

Gorilla trekking in Bwindi is consistently rated among the top wildlife encounters on Earth. Each morning, small groups of up to eight visitors — led by armed rangers and experienced trackers — set off through the dense forest to locate one of the habituated gorilla families.

Treks can last anywhere from one to eight hours depending on where the gorillas have moved. When you reach the family, you are allowed one precious hour in their presence — an hour that travellers consistently describe as the most moving experience of their lives. Silverbacks beat their chests, playful infants tumble through the undergrowth, and mothers nurse their young just metres from where you stand.

"To sit in the forest and have a mountain gorilla look you directly in the eyes is to understand in an instant what it truly means to be alive on this planet."

Gorilla Trekking Sectors

Bwindi's four trekking sectors each offer unique landscapes and access to different habituated families:

  • Buhoma — The original and most established sector in the north; home to the Mubare, Rushegura, and Habinyanja families.
  • Ruhija — Higher elevation with a cooler, more rugged trek; home to Bitukura, Oruzogo, and Kyaguliro families.
  • Rushaga — The southern hub with the highest concentration of habituated gorilla families, including Nshongi and Mishaya.
  • Nkuringo — Perched on a dramatic ridge overlooking the Virunga volcanoes and Congo basin, home to the Nkuringo family.

What You Can Experience

Beyond meeting the great apes, Bwindi provides an expansive palette of wilderness activities and rare wildlife encounters:

  • Mountain Gorilla Trekking

    Spend one unforgettable hour in the presence of a habituated wild mountain gorilla family deep in the equatorial rainforest.

  • Albertine Rift Bird Watching

    Spot 350+ bird species with specialist guides, including 23 Albertine Rift endemics such as the African green broadbill and Shelley's crimsonwing.

  • Nature Walks & Munyaga Waterfall Hikes

    Follow scenic trails through giant tree ferns and moss-draped valleys ending at the dramatic cascades of Munyaga Waterfall.

  • Batwa & Bakiga Cultural Encounters

    Meet the forest's original hunter-gatherer inhabitants to learn about traditional herbal medicine, ancient fire-making, and ancestral lore.

  • Forest Wildlife Encounters

    Encounter forest elephants, unhabituated chimpanzee troops, L'Hoest's monkeys, black-and-white colobus, and African buffalos across the ridges.

  • World-Class Forest Photography

    Capture dramatic portraits of silverbacks, misty highland ridges, and exotic macro flora in rich tropical light.

Best Time to Visit Bwindi

Bwindi can be visited year-round. The two primary dry seasons — June to August and December to February — offer the easiest trekking conditions with drier, firmer trails. The wet seasons (March to May and September to November) bring lush green foliage, active birdlife, and quieter trails with fewer visitors.

Permit Advice: Gorilla permits are strictly regulated and should be booked well in advance. Horizon Tours and Travel Tours handles all permit reservations as part of our safari packages. Permits are currently USD 800 for foreign non-residents and USD 700 for foreign residents.

Getting to Bwindi

Bwindi is located roughly 530 km southwest of Kampala. The overland journey is a scenic 8–10 hour drive through the rolling green terraced hills of western Uganda and the Kigezi highlands. Alternatively, daily scheduled and private charter flights operate from Entebbe International Airport to Kihihi or Kisoro airstrips, cutting travel time to just 90 minutes.

Where to Stay

Bwindi offers an exceptional selection of accommodations, ranging from intimate luxury eco-lodges perched above the cloud line to comfortable community cottages:

  1. Bwindi Lodge — Luxury lodge situated right at the edge of the forest in Buhoma with sweeping canopy views.
  2. Sanctuary Gorilla Forest Camp — An exclusive luxury tented camp nestled deep inside the national park boundary.
  3. Mahogany Springs — Elegant riverside cottages set along the Munyanga River with first-class hospitality.
  4. Rushaga Gorilla Camp — Ideal mid-range and luxury base for trekking the southern gorilla families.
  5. Community Rest Camps — Authentic, eco-friendly guesthouses run by local community conservation trusts.

Why Visit Bwindi?

Bwindi is one of the planet's few remaining primordial sanctuaries where human beings can look into the gentle, expressive eyes of a wild mountain gorilla in its natural kingdom. An ancient forest continuously alive since the Ice Age, it offers a transformative wildlife journey that touches every traveller deeply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before visiting Bwindi

  • Gorilla trekking is rated moderate to strenuous. You will hike on steep, muddy forest trails for anywhere from 1 to 8 hours. A reasonable level of fitness is required. Age minimum is 15 years. If you have mobility challenges, speak to us — porter services are available to assist with the trek.

  • As of 2024, gorilla permits in Uganda cost USD 800 per person per trek for foreign non-residents, USD 700 for foreign residents, and UGX 250,000 for East African citizens. Permits must be arranged in advance — HAK Tours handles all permit bookings as part of your safari package.

  • The best times are during the two dry seasons: June to August, and December to February. Trails are drier and easier to navigate. However, Bwindi is open year-round and the wet seasons (March–May and September–November) offer lush scenery, fewer visitors, and lower accommodation rates.

  • Wear long-sleeved shirts and long trousers (for protection from nettles and insects). Bring sturdy, waterproof hiking boots, garden gloves, gaiters, a rain jacket, and a change of clothes. You'll need to carry water, a snack, and a small backpack. Camera equipment should be in a waterproof bag. No flash photography is permitted near the gorillas.
