Kibale National Park

Africa's Primate Capital — Where Chimpanzees Rule the Ancient Forest

Plan Your Safari
Back to Destinations

Kibale National Park is Africa's premier primate capital — a lush equatorial rainforest in the shadow of the Rwenzori Mountains harboring the continent's greatest diversity and density of primates. Thirteen primate species call this canopy home, including over 1,400 wild chimpanzees tracked on guided excursions into the ancient forest.

The Primate Capital — Africa's highest primate density across 795 km²

About Kibale National Park

Kibale National Park covers 795 km² of moist evergreen rainforest and woodland in western Uganda, set between the dramatic Rwenzori Mountains to the west and the Rift Valley crater lakes to the south. At elevations ranging from 1,100 to 1,600 metres above sea level, the park enjoys a cool, misty climate that sustains an extraordinarily complex and unbroken ecological canopy.

Designated a national park in 1993, Kibale protects one of East Africa's most intact tropical forest blocks. Together with the adjacent community-managed Bigodi Wetland Sanctuary, Kibale forms an ecological sanctuary where chimpanzees, rare forest monkeys, and vibrant birdlife flourish side by side.

Kibale boasts the highest density and diversity of primates in all of Africa: 13 distinct species, including a thriving population of over 1,400 chimpanzees.

Chimpanzee Tracking — Africa's Finest Encounter

Kibale is globally renowned as the ultimate destination for chimpanzee tracking. The Kanyanchu Tourism Centre serves as the springboard for excursions — small groups of up to six visitors head into the lush forest accompanied by veteran Uganda Wildlife Authority trackers and guides to locate habituated chimpanzee communities.

The experience is wildly dynamic and expressive. Chimpanzees share 98.7% of our DNA, and watching them swing gracefully through towering trees, groom one another with tender care, and unleash booming pant-hoot calls through the canopy is one of the most thrilling wildlife encounters in Africa.

"Chimpanzees share 98.7% of our DNA — and spending time with a wild community in Kibale makes that connection feel not like a statistic, but like a truth written in your bones."

Chimpanzee Habituation Experience (CHEX)

For passionate primate enthusiasts, Kibale also offers the rare Chimpanzee Habituation Experience (CHEX). This full-day programme allows visitors to spend sunrise to sunset with researchers and trackers following a community undergoing habituation, witnessing their entire daily routine from waking in dawn nests to feeding, playing, and building new nests at dusk.

What You Can Experience

From primate tracking to nocturnal forest walks and community papyrus explorations, Kibale delivers immersive wilderness adventures:

  • Habituated Chimpanzee Tracking

    Embark on guided morning or afternoon treks through dense rainforest to observe playful, expressive wild chimpanzee troops up close.

  • Full-Day Habituation Experience (CHEX)

    Accompany field researchers from dawn until dusk following an unhabituated community as they forage, interact, and nest in the canopy.

  • Bigodi Wetland Sanctuary Boardwalk

    Tour a renowned community-managed papyrus swamp teeming with red colobus monkeys, great blue turacos, and elusive papyrus gonoleks.

  • Albertine Rift Bird Watching

    Spot 375+ bird species with expert local guides, searching for rare canopy jewels like the Green-breasted pitta and African grey parrot.

  • 13-Primate Forest Walks

    Encounter black-and-white colobus, Ugandan mangabeys, red-tailed monkeys, and L'Hoest's monkeys along lush shaded forest paths.

  • Guided Nocturnal Night Walks

    Venture into the darkened jungle with spotlights to discover wide-eyed pottos, bushbabies, nocturnal nightjars, and bioluminescent fungi.

Best Time to Visit Kibale

Kibale can be visited year-round. The two drier periods — June to August and December to February — offer firmer trails and ideal conditions for chimp tracking. During the wet months (March to May and September to November), the rainforest is richly verdant, fruiting trees bring primates lower down in the canopy, and birding reaches its seasonal peak.

Getting There

Kibale National Park is situated approximately 320 km west of Kampala. The drive takes 4.5 to 5.5 hours along smooth tarmac through Mubende and the picturesque, rolling tea plantations of Fort Portal. Kibale is ideally located on Uganda's western circuit, just 1.5 hours north of Queen Elizabeth National Park and 4 hours from Bwindi.

Why Visit Kibale?

Kibale is undisputed as the primate capital of the world. No other forest on the continent brings you so close to our wild cousins while surrounding you with 13 primate species and the gentle mist of equatorial rainforest.

  • Over 1,400 wild chimpanzees with 98% tracking success
  • Highest diversity and density of primates in Africa (13 species)
  • 375+ bird species including Albertine Rift endemics
  • Inspiring community-led conservation at Bigodi Wetland

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Kibale and chimp tracking

  • Chimpanzee tracking permits cost USD 250 per person for foreign non-residents, USD 200 for foreign residents, and UGX 150,000 for East African citizens. The Chimpanzee Habituation Experience (full day) costs USD 300 per person. HAK Tours arranges all permits as part of your package.

  • While gorilla trekking allows one hour with a gorilla family, chimp tracking in Kibale can be more dynamic and unpredictable — chimps move fast and high through the canopy. You may spend 2–4 hours with the group. Both experiences are extraordinary but very different in character.

  • Bigodi is a community-owned and managed wetland adjacent to Kibale, renowned for its bird life (including the Papyrus gonolek and Great blue turaco), primates (red colobus, L'Hoest's monkey, black-and-white colobus), and butterflies. A 3-hour guided walk is highly recommended and supports local livelihoods.

  • Kibale is perfectly positioned between Bwindi (gorillas, 3–4 hours south) and Murchison Falls (5–6 hours north). It is most frequently combined with Queen Elizabeth National Park (1.5 hours south) and Bwindi in 6–10 day Uganda safaris.

  • The minimum age for chimpanzee tracking is 12 years. Children aged 12–15 may participate if accompanied by a parent or guardian. The Bigodi Wetland walk is suitable for all ages and is a wonderful family activity.
